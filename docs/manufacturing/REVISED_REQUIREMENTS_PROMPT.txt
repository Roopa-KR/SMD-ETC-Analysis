# Project: Transform the Truck-Based Web App into a Tank Manufacturing, Work Center Management, and Quality Inspection System

## Objective

Analyze both provided files thoroughly and transform the existing Flask web application from a truck/shipment-oriented labor management and Estimated Time of Completion (ETC) system into a tank manufacturing, work center management, production planning, and quality inspection system.

The solution is being proposed to **Siemens Energy** as a professional, customer-facing proof of concept (PoC).

The objective is not simply to rename trucks as tanks. Understand the existing application, extract the actual manufacturing and inspection requirements from the provided files, and adapt the UI, backend, database, production workflow, calculations, and workforce management logic consistently.

The final application should demonstrate how manufacturing managers and supervisors can monitor tank production, track Critical-to-Quality (CTQ) requirements, identify bottlenecks, optimize workforce allocation, estimate production completion times, and make informed operational decisions.

If information required to build a functional demonstration is missing, generate logically consistent synthetic data. Clearly distinguish source-defined information, derived calculations, and synthetic demo assumptions.

## 8. Production Deadlines, ETC, Bottlenecks, and At-Risk Status

Replace truck departure times and pickup cutoffs with manufacturing-specific deadlines, stage deadlines, and planned completion times.

Do not reuse the truck ETC formula without validating whether it is appropriate for tank manufacturing.

### A. Required inputs

Use available or explicitly synthesized inputs such as:

- Remaining workload or production quantity.
- Historical or demo production rate.
- Work center capacity.
- Available and eligible operators.
- Shift schedules and remaining working time.
- Planned completion deadline.
- Inspection or other modeled delays.
- Dependencies and downstream constraints.

### B. Manufacturing-specific calculations

Document the formulas, units, assumptions, and data sources.

For a simplified demonstration, an estimated remaining production time may be calculated as:

Estimated Production Time = Remaining Workload / Effective Production Rate

Where the data supports this calculation, effective production rate may be derived from the applicable work center capacity, operator availability, and validated productivity assumptions.

Estimated completion must also account for shift boundaries, remaining working time, required inspection time, and explicitly modeled delays when applicable.

Do not divide workload by an arbitrary rate or use inconsistent units.

For example, if a synthetic tank has 120 units of remaining workload and its assigned resources can process 30 units per hour, the estimated production time is 4 productive hours before accounting for additional modeled delays or nonworking time.

### C. At-risk status

Determine at-risk status by comparing estimated completion against the applicable production deadline and remaining available time.

Where appropriate, distinguish:

- On track.
- At risk.
- Delayed.
- Blocked.
- Completed.

Define each status explicitly and ensure that the same underlying conditions produce consistent results across the dashboard, table, and detail pages.

Do not use truck departure or pickup cutoff logic unless an equivalent manufacturing requirement is explicitly defined.

### D. Bottleneck identification

Identify potential bottlenecks based on evidence such as:

- Workload exceeding available capacity.
- Insufficient qualified operators.
- High queue volume.
- Downstream dependencies.
- Inspection delays.
- Production rates below required rates.
- Completion estimates exceeding planned deadlines.

Explain why a work center or tank is at risk and identify possible corrective actions.
