{{ ui.panel('SUPERVISOR AWARENESS', 'Hydration and heat safety', 'Information to support awareness. It does not diagnose anyone or replace occupational-health or safety advice.') }} {{ flash_here('heat-safety') }}
  • Water close to the work. Provide cool drinking water and encourage people to drink regularly, not only when thirsty. NIOSH suggests about 1 cup (8 oz) every 15–20 minutes for moderate work in heat lasting under 2 hours, and generally no more than 6 cups an hour.
  • Electrolytes for long sweating. When sweating lasts several hours, NIOSH and OSHA point to drinks with electrolytes. Coconut water or electrolyte drinks are options to evaluate under your workplace guidance; they are not mandatory and not a proven productivity measure.
  • Rest and recovery. Allow rest and water breaks when someone feels heat discomfort, in a cooler place away from heat sources. OSHA: "In hot conditions, skipping breaks is not safe!"
  • New and returning workers. Build up time in heat gradually (NIOSH: over 7–14 days) and give unacclimatized people lighter work and more frequent rest.
  • Cutoffs never override safety. Do not ask operators to keep working in unsafe heat to meet a truck cutoff. Escalate the cutoff instead (temporary operators, loading or departure changes, customer communication).
  • Temperature and humidity are not a heat-stress measurement. Heat stress also depends on radiant heat, air movement, workload and clothing. Where a heat-safety assessment is needed, use an on-site WBGT meter or a qualified occupational-health assessment.

Sources: NIOSH workplace recommendations · OSHA Water. Rest. Shade. · OSHA heat hazard recognition (WBGT)
